This project is a poetic inquiry into the deconstruction of place and an exploration of its future ruins.
Nisyros  36°35’12.1”N 27°10’01.9”E
The investigation did not follow a linear trajectory. Instead, it unfolded through continuous shifts of
perspective between the fragment and the whole, and from the whole back to the fragment.

Why an island?

The island becomes the starting point of the investigation. It is a place of separation and isolation, but also a place of possibility and rebeginning.

Ruggiero Romano describes the island as a laboratory: a bounded territory in which processes can become visible.

Gilles Deleuze, in Desert Islands, describes the island through the idea of separation and rebeginning.

Therefore, the island.

Nisyros becomes the terrain of the investigation — a living landscape where geological time, history, culture, memory and non-human life coexist.

The circle of interest expands across space and time, gradually transforming the island into a map of deconstruction.
