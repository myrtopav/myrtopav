Why an island?
For Ruggiero Romano, an island is a space of experimentation—a laboratory where specific economic and social phenomena can be observed in their most essential forms, often isolated from the complexity of larger continental environments.

At the same time, islands are often associated with the idea of the utopian place, occupying a unique position in the human imagination that wavers between escape, tranquility, and inner exploration.

In literature, as a reflection of the human imagination, islands are frequently portrayed as places of both isolation and renewal.

In his essay Desert Islands, Gilles Deleuze writes: “Whether one dreams of islands with anxiety or with joy.”

Therefore, the island.
