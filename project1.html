This project is a poetic inquiry into the deconstruction of place and an exploration of its future ruins.
The investigation did not follow a linear trajectory. Instead, it unfolded through continuous shifts of
perspective between the fragment and the whole, and from the whole back to the fragment.
Why an island?
For Ruggiero Romano, an island is a space of experimentation—a laboratory where specific economic and social phenomena can be observed in their most essential forms, often isolated from the complex ity of larger continental environments.

At the same time, islands are often associated with the idea of the utopian place, occupying a unique position in the human imagination that wavers between escape, tranquility, and inner exploration.

In literature, as a reflection of the human imagination, islands are frequently portrayed as places of both isolation and renewal.

In his essay Desert Islands, Gilles Deleuze writes: “Whether one dreams of islands with anxiety or with joy matters little. To dream of islands means that one is dreaming of separation, that one is already separated, far from the continents, alone and lost—or rather, that one is beginning anew, creating anew, and starting over.”

Therefore, the island.
